# Meta Impacted Creators Alliance (MICA)

## Compilation of Reported Instagram Incidents and Platform Failure Patterns

Prepared: June 6, 2026  
Source archive: MICA Telegram collector archive, 1,531 archived records through June 7, 2026 UTC  
Purpose: Provide Instagram / Meta personnel with a consolidated, evidence-oriented summary of creator reports, timelines, recurring abuse patterns, and requested review areas.

## Important Evidence Note

This file is a compilation of reports made by MICA members in the group archive. Unless otherwise stated, each incident should be treated as "reported by the affected creator" and not independently verified by Meta or by a court. The archive contains message IDs, screenshots, documents, videos, URLs, and daily summaries that can be supplied as supporting material. This file is meant to help Instagram quickly identify patterns, affected accounts, and the exact areas requiring human review.

## Executive Summary

MICA was formed after multiple Instagram creators reported severe account-level harm tied to false or abusive copyright reports, account integrity suspensions, impersonation campaigns, broken or opaque appeal flows, and an informal market of paid "unban" or strike-removal services.

The issue is not limited to one account. The archive contains 42 unique senders, 164 evidence-bearing records, and 137 records with attachments. The most prevalent tracked issue categories are:

- Disabled, banned, or account integrity restrictions: 183 records.
- Fake copyright / IP strikes: 143 records.
- Appeals and Meta support failures: 109 records.
- Forced login, password reset, or account access loops: 68 records.
- Coordination, outreach, and evidence-building: 28 records.
- Unban market / scam-risk discussion: 25 records.

The central reported failure pattern is that bad actors can allegedly submit false copyright or impersonation/integrity signals that cause takedowns, strikes, suspensions, reduced reach, or permanent account disablement before the affected creator gets meaningful review. Several creators report that normal appeal paths are slow, confusing, ineffective, or risky. Several also report being asked for money by the same or related parties who caused the strikes.

## What MICA Is Asking Instagram To Review

1. Review the false copyright/IP reporting pipeline for abuse by non-rightsholders, disposable email addresses, impersonated rights holders, and Meta Rights Manager misuse.
2. Review account integrity suspensions where coordinated impersonation, false reporting, or linked-account association may have triggered automated disablement.
3. Review appeal flows that allegedly redirect to dead pages, provide no confirmation, create no actionable support record, or convert suspended accounts into permanently disabled accounts without human consideration.
4. Review Meta Verified / paid support cases where creators report paying for enhanced support but only being told to use standard appeal forms.
5. Review whether copyright strikes and integrity flags continue to suppress reach, reels-tab visibility, monetization, or external discovery after the visible issue is supposedly resolved.
6. Review screenshots and documents attached in the MICA archive for claimant emails, notices, appeal responses, takedown notices, extortion demands, and account-status screens.
7. Provide a reliable escalation path for creators with large audiences, clear ownership evidence, and documented reports of fraud/extortion.

## Core Pattern 1: False Copyright / IP Strikes Used As an Attack Vector

Multiple creators report that copyright claims were submitted by people who did not own the content, sometimes using free email addresses or impersonating the real rights holder. Reported examples include claims filed under names of creators, brands, lawyers, or clients who allegedly did not authorize the claim.

Creators report that the false claims can:

- Remove posts or ads.
- Create strikes against the account.
- Trigger account suspension or disablement after multiple claims.
- Leave the account in a fragile state even after claims are retracted.
- Reduce reach or engagement after the visible strike is removed.
- Encourage creators to archive or delete reels to avoid future attacks.

Representative member reports:

- @TheMcFreakery reported waves of false copyright strikes against @mcfreakery, despite claiming permission for reposted videos. He reported that his page was taken down and that the people behind the strikes demanded money to remove the strikes and restore the account. Evidence markers include multiple screenshot attachments and Instagram reel links. Source: May 5, message 39; evidence messages 40-46.
- @richardconway reported receiving 10 false strikes in one week, allegedly from impersonated rights holders using Hotmail addresses. He said the account was suspended, later restored after appeals, and remained fragile. Source: May 6, messages 56, 59, 62, 786, 1065.
- @honorable reported false strikes that took down @futuretech and a strike on @robert for a personal elephant photo. He also reported unresolved ViralHog-related claims despite permission from the clip owner and a paid retraction fee. Source: May 6, messages 54, 58, 291, 300.
- @mrgusx / Brenden reported that @liveforclass and two other pages were being struck in the Live For Class name and email, even though he owns the account, and that he had to delete all reels and shift toward AI content to reduce strike risk. Source: May 7, message 475.
- @LukeGosseling reported @mindsetraptors with 1.5M followers was struck by paulhilse@outlook.com after posting an ad, then taken down. He also reported other pages affected by copyright or integrity issues, including @forthinkingminds, @hustling.millionaires, createimprovement, @girlieceo, and @wifi.hustlers. Source: May 9, message 737.
- @manojkumardahiya reported @millionairestutor with 600K followers was hit by a strike from economyarab21@proton.me after a credited Johnny Harris reel, then later hit by fake claims using names of business contacts/clients such as @savetoinvest and @billionairedirector. Source: May 8, messages 583, 586, 594, 599.

## Core Pattern 4: Broken, Slow, or Risky Appeals

MICA members repeatedly report that appeal paths are confusing, slow, ineffective, or risky. Several members believe an appeal can worsen the outcome by converting a suspension into permanent disablement or making paid/unofficial restoration harder.

Representative member reports:

- @richardconway reported appeals took exactly two weeks and only some false strikes were clearly removed. Source: May 6, message 56; May 10, message 786; May 13, message 1065.
- @Ollehellman reported that trying to appeal a fake copyright claim sent him to a Help Center page. Source: May 7, message 452.
- Kahan Modi reported that the copyright appeal link returned "Sorry, this content is not available right now." Source: May 13, message 1047.
- @ralphklipper reported no confirmation after submitting an appeal and being redirected to another page. Source: May 17, message 1105.
- @amex warned against appealing, saying the account may be permanently disabled right after. Source: May 7, messages 469-470; June 4, message 1432.
- @mehdiddi reported appealing and having the account disabled, with the main account permanently disabled while one account came back. Source: May 20, messages 1133, 1135.
- @jacobv reported using appeal forms and Meta Verified support but being told to keep using forms. Source: May 10, message 781; May 17, message 1116.
- @igdann explicitly held off on the in-app appeal because he did not trust the same automated system to consider new evidence. Source: May 27, message 1369.

## Core Pattern 5: Support Channels and Meta Verified Not Resolving the Problem

Creators report that normal support and paid support do not provide meaningful investigation, even where the issue involves extortion, impersonation, false claimants, or high-value business accounts.

Representative member reports:

- @richardconway reported paying for Meta Verified Max at about $400/month and still receiving support that could only tell him to appeal. Source: May 6, message 62.
- @jacobv reported Meta Verified support only told him to appeal using forms. Source: May 10, message 781.
- @hamid_elmoussaoui reported subscribing to Meta Verified, opening more than 22 support tickets, providing original emails, phone numbers, screenshots, and proof of ownership, and still receiving no meaningful resolution. Source: May 17, message 1119.
- @amex reported an open support ticket with no update and later that Meta verification expired while a support ticket was ongoing. Source: May 7, message 503; June 3, message 1418.

## Core Pattern 6: Forced Login / Password / Security Events

The archive contains repeated concern about account security events, login codes, forced password changes, and new methods allegedly enabling account access.

Representative member reports:

Members report that even when visible account status appears normal, strikes may reduce engagement, reels visibility, external follower discovery, or creator confidence.

## Requested Instagram / Meta Actions

1. Assign a human trust-and-safety reviewer to the MICA evidence bundle.
2. Provide a secure intake channel where affected creators can submit:
   - Instagram username(s).
   - Account ID(s), if available.
   - Strike/takedown notice IDs.
   - Claimant email addresses and claimant names.
   - Screenshots of notices and appeal results.
   - Proof of ownership or permission.
   - Evidence of extortion or claimant contact.
3. Audit repeat claimant emails, disposable domains, and claimant identities appearing across affected accounts.
4. Audit whether false claimants are using Meta Rights Manager or copyright-match tools to upload stolen content and then report the original or authorized poster.
5. Add pre-enforcement review or higher-friction verification before repeated copyright reports can disable large accounts or business-critical pages.
6. Allow affected creators to appeal before strikes become account-ending enforcement events, especially when the claimant uses a free email address or claims to represent a major brand/person without proof.
7. Provide appeal confirmations, case IDs, review status, and a path to upload evidence rather than sending creators to generic Help Center pages.
8. Prevent appeal submission from automatically increasing disablement risk.
9. Review account integrity bans linked to impersonation attacks, Account Center association, forced login events, and coordinated false reporting.
10. Investigate whether post-strike reach suppression or Reels visibility limits persist after a false strike is retracted or removed.
11. Coordinate with law-enforcement-friendly reporting channels where extortion, impersonation, or blackmail is documented.
