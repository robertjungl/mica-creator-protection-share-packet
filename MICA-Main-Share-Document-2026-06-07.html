Executive Summary

Multiple unrelated creators report a similar failure pattern on Instagram: questionable copyright reports or impersonation events trigger strikes, account restrictions, account removals, or reach suppression; normal appeal routes are slow, confusing, or ineffective; and some creators report demands for payment to retract claims or restore accounts.

The concern is not one isolated account dispute. The archive shows repeated reports across creator categories, including meme pages, auto pages, business and finance pages, motivational pages, and personal creator accounts. Several members describe accounts with large followings becoming fragile after strikes or integrity actions, even when they believe the claim was false, abusive, or filed by someone who was not the true rights holder.

The most useful next step is a human escalation review inside Meta/Instagram that looks at the connected pattern, not just one creator at a time. The linked evidence files are intended to help reviewers see exact screenshots, emails, complainant addresses, appeal responses, and related account state where members provided them.

What We Are Asking Meta/Instagram To Do

  1. Assign a human escalation owner for the MICA cases and review them as a connected abuse pattern.
  2. Audit copyright and IP reports tied to suspicious consumer email addresses, impersonated brands, repeated claimant identities, or claimants who are not verified rights holders.
  3. Remove false or abusive strikes, restore wrongly disabled accounts and content, and reverse downstream penalties such as account integrity flags or distribution suppression where appropriate.
  4. Investigate reported extortion and paid retraction or unban markets connected to copyright takedowns and account restoration.
  5. Fix appeal flows so creators can submit evidence before enforcement escalates, receive confirmation numbers, and understand the status of each claim.
  6. Improve Meta Verified and paid support escalation so large creator accounts are not simply routed back to generic forms.
  7. Preserve relevant enforcement records, complainant data, appeal logs, and internal review notes for affected accounts.

Main Patterns Seen In The Archive

  • False or abusive copyright/IP reports by apparent non-rightsholders, impersonators, or questionable claimant email addresses.
  • Extortion or payment demands tied to strike retractions, account restoration, or unban services.
  • Account integrity bans following copyright activity, impersonation attacks, appeals, or unexplained automated enforcement.
  • Broken or weak appeal flows, including confusing responses, auto-appeal behavior, missing confirmations, and no meaningful place to submit proof.
  • Meta Verified or paid support that appears unable to solve enforcement problems even for large accounts.
  • Creator self-censorship and operational damage, including archiving reels, deleting content, changing content strategy, and treating previously stable accounts as fragile.
