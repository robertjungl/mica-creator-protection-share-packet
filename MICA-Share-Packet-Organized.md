## How To Read This

This packet is designed for two audiences: MICA members who need a clear shared picture of what has been reported, and Instagram/Meta personnel who need a practical map of the issue. It is not a final legal filing. Member claims are presented as reported by the affected creator unless separately verified.

Each case below includes:

- The affected member or sender.
- The issue patterns detected in the archive.
- A short factual summary.
- Key messages with dates and Telegram message IDs.
- Evidence files downloaded from Telegram where available.
- Additional links or evidence markers.

The companion CSV file `evidence-index.csv` lists all downloaded evidence files by sender and message ID. The folder `evidence-files/` contains the actual screenshots, documents, videos, or voice notes retrieved from Telegram.

## High-Level Pattern Summary

The archive shows repeated reports of false or abusive copyright strikes, account integrity bans, impersonation attacks, broken appeal flows, weak Meta Verified support, and an informal market of paid strike removal or unban services. The most important pattern is that many unrelated creators describe similar failure modes: false reports arrive, accounts are restricted or disabled, appeals are slow or risky, and bad actors can demand payment because they appear to control the strike/retraction process.

## Main Platform Issues To Escalate

1. False copyright/IP reports by non-rightsholders or impersonators.
2. Extortion demands tied to copyright retractions or account restoration.
3. Account integrity bans triggered by impersonation, association, or unexplained automated enforcement.
4. Appeal links and forms that fail, provide no confirmation, or do not allow meaningful evidence submission.
5. Meta Verified support repeatedly routing creators back to generic forms.
6. Potential reach/reels-tab suppression after strikes.
7. Login/security warnings and unexplained codes or forced password events.
